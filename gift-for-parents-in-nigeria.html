For the diaspora

Can’t be home for their big day? Send something they’ll play for years.

A personalised song, a family tribute wall or a photo film, made from abroad and enjoyed at home.

For many of us abroad, the hardest part of a parent's 60th, 70th or 80th isn't the cost of the gift. It's not being in the room. Money and cloth arrive, but the moment passes without us.

A personal keepsake changes that. It carries your voice, your memories and your siblings' words into the celebration, and it stays long after the party ends.

Three gifts that travel well

1. A song written from their life

Tell us where they grew up, how they raised you, the things they always say and the people they love. We turn it into an original song with their name in it. You can include Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa or Pidgin phrases. Read the full lyrics free before you pay, then hear your song within minutes.

2. A tribute wall the whole family fills

Share one link in the family WhatsApp group. Children in London, grandchildren in Houston, old friends in Abuja all add messages and photos. On the day it plays on the TV or a projector at the party, or you send the link so they can read it quietly at home.

3. A film of their life, made with you

For the milestone that deserves everything: we write the song with you, gather the family's photos through one upload link, and cut a film to the music, ready for the screen on the day. You see the plan and dates before you pay anything.

Time zones and distance aren't a problem.

Everything works on a phone, with no app and no account. Family in Nigeria only need the link. If the network is slow on the day, download the song or film beforehand and play it offline.

How to make it land on the day

  1. Gather the siblings early. Two or three weeks ahead, start the family WhatsApp thread with the wall link or the story questions.
  2. Ask one person at the venue to press play. An MC, a cousin or the event planner. Send them the file the day before.
  3. Mention the names that matter. Grandchildren's names, the hometown, the church, the nickname only family uses. That's what makes the room go quiet.
  4. Record a short video message from those abroad to play straight after the song.
